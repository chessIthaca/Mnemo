**L3 — section-injection hardening: RESOLVED, verified airtight against the parser.**
- The parser (src/workflow/plan_file.rs:194-232) has exactly two structure-switching forms, both matched on the TRIMMED line: `trimmed.starts_with("# ")` → title overwrite (:197-204), and `trimmed.strip_prefix("## ")` → section switch (:206-231, including the `## Step N — Title` step-injection arm at :219 and the new `"detailed steps"` arm at :214).
- `escape_section_markers` (src/tool/workflow/plan.rs:196-208) prefixes `\` to the RAW line whenever `line.trim_start()` starts with `## ` or `# ` — precisely those two forms. The backslash becomes the line's first non-whitespace character, so the parser's trim-then-match can never see a `#`/`##` start; prefixing the raw (not trimmed) line also covers indented markers (`    ## Bug` → `\    ## Bug`), which a trim-before-match parser would otherwise re-expose. Coverage is exact in the other direction too: `### H`, `#tag`, `##tag` match neither the parser (both forms require the space) nor the escape — benign lines are byte-identical.
- Round-trip: the DetailedSteps arm accumulates the RAW line (`push_str(line)`, plan_file.rs:287), so `\## Bug` survives re-parse into the section body, and serialize writes the body verbatim — stable across the `update_plan`/`complete_step` re-serializations, no double-escaping (the escape runs once, at creation).
- Wiring: applied to the joined bullet body after the `- ` prefixes, so single-line steps are inherently safe (they start with `- `) and every column-0 continuation line of a multi-line step is escaped — the only injectable surface. `detailed_step_count` is computed before the escape, so counts are unaffected.
- The regression test `detailed_steps_cannot_inject_sections` is genuinely red without the escape (traced: unescaped, `## Bug` flips the parser to Section::Bug and the symptom re-parses as "injected symptom"; `## Regression test` sets the finish-gate field; `# Plan: fake title` overwrites the title) and pins all of those plus the escaped lines surviving in the body.
